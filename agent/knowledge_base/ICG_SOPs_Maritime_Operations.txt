INDIAN COAST GUARD (ICG)
STANDARD OPERATING PROCEDURES FOR MARITIME OPERATIONS
HEADQUARTERS DIRECTIVE NO. ICG/OPS/SOP/2024

CHAPTER 1 — SEARCH AND RESCUE (SAR) OPERATIONS

Section 1.1 — SAR Response Framework
1. The Indian Coast Guard is the designated national maritime SAR agency under the National Maritime Search and Rescue (M-SAR) plan.
2. The Maritime Rescue Coordination Centres (MRCCs) at Mumbai, Chennai, and Port Blair coordinate all SAR operations in the Indian Search and Rescue Region (ISRR).
3. All Coast Guard vessels and aircraft shall maintain readiness to respond to SAR alerts within 30 minutes of activation.

Section 1.2 — SAR Alert Categories
1. UNCERTAINTY PHASE (INCERFA): When a vessel is overdue at its destination or has failed to make an expected position report.
2. ALERT PHASE (ALERFA): When attempts to establish contact with the vessel have failed and inquiries to other relevant sources have been unsuccessful.
3. DISTRESS PHASE (DETRESFA): When further attempts to contact the vessel and enquiries have been unsuccessful and the vessel is considered to be in distress.

Section 1.3 — SAR Operational Procedures
1. Upon receipt of a distress alert, the MRCC shall:
   (a) Acknowledge the alert;
   (b) Assess the situation and available resources;
   (c) Designate an On-Scene Coordinator (OSC);
   (d) Deploy appropriate Coast Guard assets.
2. Search patterns shall be selected based on:
   (a) Expanding square search for small search areas with known datum;
   (b) Sector search for a well-defined datum with small probable area;
   (c) Parallel track search for large search areas;
   (d) Creeping line ahead for search areas of moderate size.
3. Communication protocols: Channel 16 (156.8 MHz) shall be monitored continuously during SAR operations.

CHAPTER 2 — MARITIME PATROL AND SURVEILLANCE

Section 2.1 — Patrol Zone Assignments
1. Coast Guard Districts shall maintain patrol schedules covering their assigned Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ) sectors.
2. Patrol frequencies: High-threat areas — daily; Medium-threat — twice weekly; Routine — weekly.
3. Each patrol vessel shall carry current charts, communication equipment, and personnel trained in boarding procedures.

Section 2.2 — Maritime Domain Awareness (MDA)
1. All Coast Guard stations shall maintain the Vessel Traffic Monitoring System (VTMS) operational 24/7.
2. AIS data integration: All vessels within the Indian EEZ shall be tracked via the Automatic Identification System.
3. Suspicious activity reporting: Any vessel not transmitting AIS or exhibiting unusual behavior shall be reported immediately.

Section 2.3 — Anti-Smuggling Operations
1. Coast Guard vessels have the authority to stop, board, search, and seize vessels suspected of smuggling within territorial waters.
2. Boarding teams shall consist of a minimum of 4 personnel including 1 officer.
3. Evidence handling procedures shall comply with Indian Evidence Act requirements.

CHAPTER 3 — MARINE POLLUTION RESPONSE

Section 3.1 — Oil Spill Response Tiers
1. Tier 1 (Minor): Up to 10 tonnes — local response capability with mechanical recovery equipment.
2. Tier 2 (Medium): 10-1000 tonnes — regional coordination with additional equipment from stockpiles.
3. Tier 3 (Major): Over 1000 tonnes — national/international activation under National Oil Spill Disaster Contingency Plan (NOS-DCP).

Section 3.2 — Response Equipment Standards
1. All Coast Guard stations shall maintain minimum Tier 1 equipment including:
   (a) 200 metres of containment boom;
   (b) 1 portable skimmer system;
   (c) 5 tonnes of dispersant;
   (d) Personal protective equipment (PPE) for 10 personnel.
2. Equipment shall be tested and maintained quarterly.

Section 3.3 — Dispersant Use Authorization
1. Dispersant use requires authorization from the ICG National Contingency Plan coordinator.
2. Dispersants shall only be used when: (a) mechanical recovery is not feasible; (b) the net environmental benefit analysis favors dispersant use; (c) the oil type is amenable to dispersant application.

CHAPTER 4 — PORT AND COASTAL SECURITY

Section 4.1 — Coastal Security Operations
1. The ICG conducts coastal security patrols in coordination with the Indian Navy and state marine police.
2. All fishing vessels over 20 metres shall carry identification equipment (transponder/AIS).
3. Community Interaction Programmes (CIP) shall be conducted in all coastal villages within the jurisdiction.

Section 4.2 — Port Facility Security Assessment
1. All ports within ICG jurisdiction shall have an approved Port Facility Security Plan (PFSP) under the ISPS Code.
2. Security levels: Level 1 (normal), Level 2 (heightened), Level 3 (exceptional).
3. Drills shall be conducted at least every 3 months, and exercises at least once a year.
