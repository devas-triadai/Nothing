INTERNATIONAL MARITIME ORGANIZATION (IMO)
INTERNATIONAL CONVENTION FOR THE SAFETY OF LIFE AT SEA (SOLAS)
CHAPTER II-1: CONSTRUCTION — STRUCTURE, SUBDIVISION AND STABILITY, MACHINERY AND ELECTRICAL INSTALLATIONS

PART A — GENERAL

Regulation 1 — Application
1. Unless expressly provided otherwise, this chapter shall apply to ships the keels of which are laid or which are at a similar stage of construction on or after 1 January 2009.
2. For the purpose of this chapter, the term "similar stage of construction" means the stage at which construction identifiable with a specific ship begins and assembly of that ship has commenced comprising at least 50 tonnes or 1 percent of the estimated mass of all structural material, whichever is the less.

Regulation 2 — Definitions
1. "Subdivision load line" means a waterline used in determining the subdivision of the ship.
2. "Deepest subdivision load line" is the waterline which corresponds to the greatest draught permitted by the subdivision requirements which are applicable.
3. "Length of the ship" means the length measured between perpendiculars taken at the extremities of the deepest subdivision load line.
4. "Breadth of the ship" means the extreme moulded breadth at or below the deepest subdivision load line.
5. "Draught" means the vertical distance from the moulded baseline at midships to the subdivision load line in question.
6. "Bulkhead deck" means the uppermost deck up to which the transverse watertight bulkheads and the ship's side are carried.
7. "Margin line" is a line drawn at least 76 mm below the upper surface of the bulkhead deck at side.

PART B — SUBDIVISION, STABILITY AND RELATED ARRANGEMENTS

Regulation 5 — Subdivision and Damage Stability
1. Ships of 80 m in length and above shall comply with the probabilistic method of determination of the subdivision and damage stability.
2. The attained subdivision index A shall not be less than the required subdivision index R.
3. Required subdivision index R = 1 - (128 / (Ls + 152)) for passenger ships where Ls is the subdivision length.
4. Required subdivision index R = 1 - (128 / (Ls + 152))^(1/3) for cargo ships.

Regulation 6 — Required Subdivision Index R
1. The degree of subdivision to be provided shall be determined by the required subdivision index R.
2. For passenger ships: R = 1 - (128 / (Ls + 152)), where Ls is the subdivision length of the ship in metres.
3. For cargo ships: R = 1 - (128 / (Ls + 152))^(1/3).

Regulation 7 — Attained Subdivision Index
1. The attained subdivision index A shall be calculated by the formula: A = Σ pi × si for all cases of flooding.
2. pi is the probability that only the compartment or group of compartments under consideration may be flooded.
3. si is the probability of survival after flooding the compartment or group of compartments under consideration.

PART C — MACHINERY INSTALLATIONS

Regulation 26 — General Requirements
1. The machinery, boilers and other pressure vessels, associated piping systems and fittings shall be of a design and construction adequate for the service for which they are intended.
2. They shall be installed and protected so as to reduce to a minimum any danger to persons on board.
3. Due regard shall be paid to the materials used in the construction of essential components.

Regulation 27 — Bilge Pumping Arrangements
1. An efficient bilge pumping arrangement shall be provided, capable of pumping from and draining any watertight compartment.
2. At least two power bilge pumps shall be provided, one of which may be driven by the main engines.
3. The diameter d (in mm) of the main bilge pipe shall be calculated by: d = 25 + 1.68 × √(L(B+D)), where L, B, and D are in metres.

PART D — ELECTRICAL INSTALLATIONS

Regulation 40 — Emergency Source of Electrical Power
1. A self-contained emergency source of electrical power shall be provided.
2. The emergency source of electrical power shall be capable of operating for at least 36 hours for passenger ships and 18 hours for cargo ships.
3. The emergency source may be either: (a) an accumulator (battery) or (b) a generator with independent fuel supply and starting system.
4. Emergency lighting shall be arranged to provide illumination of the embarkation areas, alleyways, stairways, and machinery spaces.

PART E — ADDITIONAL REQUIREMENTS FOR PERIODICALLY UNATTENDED MACHINERY SPACES

Regulation 46 — Fire Protection
1. Means shall be provided to detect and give alarms at an early stage in case of fire in periodically unattended machinery spaces.
2. A fixed fire-extinguishing system shall be provided in all machinery spaces.
3. The system shall be operable from outside the protected space.

Regulation 47 — Flooding Protection
1. Bilge level detectors shall be installed in all compartments that are not permanently attended.
2. An alarm shall be given at the navigating bridge when bilge water reaches a predetermined level.
3. Any valve serving a sea inlet or discharge below the waterline shall be automatically controlled.

CHAPTER II-1 APPENDIX — PROBABILISTIC DAMAGE STABILITY
1. The probabilistic concept assesses the probability of survival after collision damage.
2. It considers the contribution of each watertight compartment to the overall safety of the ship.
3. The calculation methodology provides a rational approach accounting for the actual probability of flooding.
4. Ships must demonstrate compliance through detailed calculations at the design stage and verify at construction.
