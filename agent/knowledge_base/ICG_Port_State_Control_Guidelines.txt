INDIAN COAST GUARD (ICG)
PORT STATE CONTROL GUIDELINES
ICG/PSC/GL/2024

CHAPTER 1 — PORT STATE CONTROL FRAMEWORK

Section 1.1 — Authority and Mandate
1. The Indian Coast Guard exercises Port State Control (PSC) authority under the Merchant Shipping Act, 1958 and relevant IMO conventions.
2. India is a member of the Indian Ocean Memorandum of Understanding (IO MOU) on Port State Control.
3. The IO MOU inspection target: Each member state shall inspect at least 15% of individual foreign ships entering its ports annually.

Section 1.2 — Selection Criteria for Inspection
1. Ship Targeting System: Vessels are prioritized based on a risk-based targeting system.
2. Priority I (mandatory inspection): Ships not inspected in last 6 months; ships with known deficiencies; ships from flag states on the black/grey list.
3. Priority II (may be inspected): Ships with outstanding recommendations; ships of a type subject to PSC concentration inspection campaigns.
4. Override factors: Ship involved in incident; crew complaint; information from other parties.

CHAPTER 2 — INSPECTION PROCEDURES

Section 2.1 — Initial Inspection
1. The PSC inspector (PSCI) shall examine:
   (a) Validity of certificates and documents;
   (b) Overall condition of the ship;
   (c) Competence of crew;
   (d) Compliance with applicable conventions.
2. Certificates to be verified: (a) International Tonnage Certificate; (b) International Load Line Certificate; (c) Safety Construction Certificate; (d) Safety Equipment Certificate; (e) Safety Radio Certificate; (f) IOPP Certificate; (g) ISM Code Document of Compliance and Safety Management Certificate; (h) ISPS Code International Ship Security Certificate; (i) IAPP Certificate; (j) BWM Certificate.

Section 2.2 — Expanded Inspection
1. An expanded inspection shall be triggered when clear grounds exist that the condition of the ship or its equipment does not correspond substantially with the particulars of any of the certificates.
2. Clear grounds include: (a) absence of required equipment; (b) evidence of deficiency in safety equipment; (c) absence of working oil discharge monitoring system; (d) absence of valid certificates; (e) crew unable to communicate with each other or with PSC inspector.

Section 2.3 — Detailed Inspection
1. A detailed inspection involves examination of specific areas: (a) structural condition; (b) fire safety measures; (c) ISM compliance; (d) working and living conditions; (e) navigation safety equipment.
2. Each area shall be assessed against the relevant convention requirements.

CHAPTER 3 — DEFICIENCY CODES AND ACTIONS

Section 3.1 — Deficiency Classification
1. Deficiency codes follow the IMO/IO MOU harmonized system.
2. Major categories:
   (a) 01000 — Certificates and documentation;
   (b) 02000 — Structural condition;
   (c) 03000 — Water/weathertight integrity;
   (d) 04000 — Emergency systems;
   (e) 05000 — Radio communications;
   (f) 06000 — Cargo operations;
   (g) 07000 — Fire safety;
   (h) 08000 — Alarms;
   (i) 09000 — Working and living conditions;
   (j) 10000 — Safety of navigation;
   (k) 11000 — Life-saving appliances;
   (l) 12000 — Dangerous goods;
   (m) 13000 — Propulsion and auxiliary machinery;
   (n) 14000 — Pollution prevention;
   (o) 15000 — ISM;
   (p) 16000 — ISPS;
   (q) 17000 — Other.

Section 3.2 — Action Codes
1. Code 10 — Deficiency rectified
2. Code 15 — Rectify deficiency at next port
3. Code 16 — Rectify within 14 days
4. Code 17 — Master informed
5. Code 30 — Detainable deficiency (ship detained until rectified)
6. Code 40 — Next port of call informed
7. Code 45 — Referred to flag State
8. Code 50 — Ship banned from operating
9. Code 85 — Investigation of possible contravention

Section 3.3 — Detention Criteria
1. A ship shall be detained when deficiencies are sufficiently serious to merit a port State control officer returning to the ship to verify that they have been rectified before the ship sails.
2. Grounds for detention include:
   (a) Inoperative fire detection or extinguishing equipment;
   (b) Non-functional navigation equipment (radar, gyro, steering);
   (c) Insufficient or unqualified crew;
   (d) Structural deficiencies affecting seaworthiness;
   (e) Missing or expired certificates;
   (f) Oil pollution prevention equipment failure.
3. Detention notification: Flag State, next port of call, and IO MOU secretariat shall be notified within 24 hours.

CHAPTER 4 — CONCENTRATED INSPECTION CAMPAIGNS (CIC)

Section 4.1 — Annual CIC Topics
1. The IO MOU conducts one Concentrated Inspection Campaign per year focusing on a specific topic.
2. Recent CIC topics include: STCW compliance, stability instruments, fire safety, ballast water management, MARPOL Annex VI compliance.
3. During CICs, all inspected ships shall be checked for compliance with the campaign questionnaire in addition to the normal inspection scope.
4. CIC results are reported to the IO MOU secretariat and published in the annual report.
